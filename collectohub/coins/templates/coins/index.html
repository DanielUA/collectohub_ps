About Me - My Coin Collecting Journey

Hello, I'm Danyil, and I've been passionate about coin collecting since childhood. My journey began with a few foreign coins that my mother had, which sparked my interest in numismatics. Over time, I shared this hobby with my classmates, and we would swap coins with each other. I spent many days going to markets and flea markets, searching for interesting finds to add to my collection.

I bought my first coin album and started collecting coins from various places, even purchasing them through local classifieds. Then, with the advent of the internet, my hobby evolved. I gained access to online catalogs, which helped me sort coins by century, continent, and country. I also started purchasing coins from specialized auctions and online platforms, attending collector meetups, and eventually acquiring my first coin album with holders for my growing collection.

As my collection grew, I began buying and reselling coins. I also started purchasing coins directly from the national bank, including participating in an auction for limited-edition coins. Although I didn't win the auction, I was surprised at how much the coins were resold for afterward—sometimes at a significantly higher price than what they were initially going for.

Unfortunately, due to the war in Ukraine, I had to leave much of my collection behind when I moved to the UK. But once I settled here, I decided to pick up my passion for coin collecting again. I even learned programming and created a website, CollectoHub, dedicated to coin exchanges among collectors.

I also had the opportunity to attend a coin exhibition in London, where I distributed leaflets about my website and met some fascinating people. From time to time, I win storage units at auctions, and in one memorable instance, I found a suitcase filled with various items, including a small bag with around 100 coins—about 20 of them were silver, dating back to the late 19th and early 20th century from old England.

My journey with coins has been full of surprises, and I'm always eager to share new discoveries and experiences with fellow collectors. This site will serve as a space for me to document and share all things related to coin collecting, from historical pieces to modern finds, and my passion for keeping this wonderful hobby alive.

I'm excited to have you join me on this journey, and I hope you find inspiration, knowledge, and a community here!

Clean & Save Your Coins

Throughout my coin collecting journey, I've gained a fair amount of experience with cleaning and preserving coins. I'll share with you some of the methods I've tried over the years and what I've learned through trial and error.

The Beginning

My first attempt at cleaning coins was simple yet quite primitive—I would rub the coin against smooth, clean wood. It was a rather interesting way to kill time, though not particularly effective, and it left me with sore fingers!

Chemical Cleaning and Other Methods

As I continued collecting, I began experimenting with different household cleaning agents. I also tried using electrolysis, and at one point, I used more aggressive chemicals. Unfortunately, some coins didn't survive these experiments, which taught me valuable lessons about the dangers of certain methods.

What Works Best?

After much trial and error, I settled on using a rust remover solution. This method has proven to be more effective and gentle on the coins, preserving their value and appearance. Here are a few things I've learned over the years:
Sort Coins by Metal Type

It's crucial to categorize coins based on their metal composition before cleaning. Brass, copper, and nickel coins are the easiest and safest to clean. Aluminum coins require a different approach, and zinc coins are the most challenging—they can be easily damaged if not treated with care.

Experiment, but Cautiously

I recommend experimenting only with less rare or valuable coins. Some cleaning methods, such as using harsh chemicals, can severely damage the coins if left in the solution for too long. It's better to repeat the cleaning process than overexpose the coin to any chemical.

Never Mix Metals in the Same Solution

Always clean coins of different metal types separately. Mixing metals in one solution can cause undesirable reactions and damage to the coins.
