My Coin Collecting Journey

The story behind CollectoHub

Hello, I'm Danyil

I've been passionate about coin collecting since childhood. My journey began with a few foreign coins that my mother had, which sparked my interest in numismatics. Over time, I shared this hobby with my classmates, and we would swap coins with each other.

I bought my first coin album and started collecting coins from various places, even purchasing them through local classifieds. Then, with the advent of the internet, my hobby evolved. I gained access to online catalogs, which helped me sort coins by century, continent, and country.

As my collection grew, I began buying and reselling coins. I also started purchasing coins directly from the national bank, including participating in an auction for limited-edition coins.

Unfortunately, due to the war in Ukraine, I had to leave much of my collection behind when I moved to the UK. But once I settled here, I decided to pick up my passion for coin collecting again. I even learned programming and created a website, CollectoHub, dedicated to coin exchanges among collectors.

I also had the opportunity to attend a coin exhibition in London, where I distributed leaflets about my website and met some fascinating people. From time to time, I win storage units at auctions, and in one memorable instance, I found a suitcase filled with various items, including a small bag with around 100 coins—about 20 of them were silver, dating back to the late 19th and early 20th century from old England.

My journey with coins has been full of surprises, and I'm always eager to share new discoveries and experiences with fellow collectors. This site will serve as a space for me to document and share all things related to coin collecting, from historical pieces to modern finds, and my passion for keeping this wonderful hobby alive.

Coin Care & Preservation

Expert tips to maintain your collection's value

Lessons From Years of Experience

Throughout my coin collecting journey, I've gained a fair amount of experience with cleaning and preserving coins. Here's what I've learned through trial and error:

It's crucial to categorize coins based on their metal composition before cleaning. Brass, copper, and nickel coins are the easiest and safest to clean. Aluminum coins require a different approach, and zinc coins are the most challenging—they can be easily damaged if not treated with care.

I recommend experimenting only with less rare or valuable coins. Some cleaning methods, such as using harsh chemicals, can severely damage the coins if left in the solution for too long. It's better to repeat the cleaning process than overexpose the coin to any chemical.

Always clean coins of different metal types separately. Mixing metals in one solution can cause undesirable reactions and damage to the coins.
